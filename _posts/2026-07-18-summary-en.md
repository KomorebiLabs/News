The Trump administration is considering establishing an independent AI regulatory body similar to the Financial Industry Regulatory Authority (FINRA) to vet top-tier models. This initiative aims to address cybersecurity concerns from Wall Street and pushback from Silicon Valley against temporary government controls. This proposal represents a significant shift in AI governance by introducing industry-led self-regulation under government oversight, balancing safety with innovation. It directly impacts major tech companies like Anthropic and OpenAI, which have previously objected to strict federal mandates. The proposed structure mirrors FINRA's relationship with the SEC, where the independent watchdog reports to the Treasury Department led by Scott Bessent. While Google DeepMind's CEO Demis Hassabis supports similar independent oversight, the plan still requires President Trump's review and may be adjusted.

**Background**: FINRA is a private, nonprofit organization that regulates brokerage firms and stockbrokers in the United States, operating under the oversight of the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC). Unlike direct government agencies, self-regulatory organizations like FINRA are funded by fees from regulated entities, offering flexibility while maintaining compliance standards. This model allows industry experts to help shape safety protocols, a concept now being applied to AI development.
