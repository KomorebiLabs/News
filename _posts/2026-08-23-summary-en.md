## [How Complex Systems Fail: A Seminal 1998 Paper on Systemic Catastrophe](https://how.complexsystems.fail/) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Charles Perrow's 1998 paper argues that root cause analysis is fundamentally misguided for complex systems, since they inherently run in degraded states with latent failures that accumulate before catastrophic events. The paper has seen renewed attention, with practitioners connecting its insights to modern practices like Chaos Engineering. This paper reshapes how engineers and organizations approach system reliability, incident analysis, and safety culture. Its core insight—that complex systems are inherently hazardous and operate in degraded mode—directly underpins modern reliability engineering and Chaos Engineering methodologies. Perrow identifies several key principles: complex systems contain changing mixtures of latent failures, they run in degraded mode, catastrophe is always imminent, and post-accident attribution to a single root cause is fundamentally wrong. The paper emphasizes that system operations are dynamic, with components compensating for each other through redundancies and human intervention.

**Background**: Charles Perrow was a sociologist known for his work on organizational accidents and normal accident theory. Complex systems are characterized by tight coupling (where components depend heavily on each other) and interactive complexity (where failures can trigger unpredictable chain reactions). These properties make it nearly impossible to predict or prevent all failures through traditional safety engineering alone.
