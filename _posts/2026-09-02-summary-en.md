## [Open-Source AI Detectors Fail to Maintain Low False-Positive Rates](https://www.reddit.com/r/MachineLearning/comments/1w58erw/most_opensource_ai_detectors_cant_hold_a_05/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A systematic benchmark of six notable open-source AI text detectors found that four cannot achieve a 0.5% false-positive rate on human web text, and all models collapse when detecting humanizer-paraphrased AI content. Additionally, every detector flags non-native English essays at higher rates than native ones, revealing a fundamental bias in the field. These findings demonstrate that current open-source AI detectors are unreliable for real-world deployment, especially against adversarial paraphrasing and non-native writing. The results challenge the assumption that such tools can accurately distinguish AI-generated text from human writing, impacting academic integrity, content moderation, and trust in automated detection systems. The benchmark used a controlled protocol with public datasets, setting each model's threshold to achieve a matched 0.5% false-positive rate on 6,930 human documents. The best-performing detector (tropha-mini) caught only 42% of humanizer-paraphrased AI text, while the second-best caught just 4%. Four models, including MAGE and the old OpenAI RoBERTa detector, could not even reach the 0.5% false-positive threshold.

reddit · r/MachineLearning · /u/grumpyp2 · Sep 2, 12:04

**Background**: AI text detectors are machine learning models designed to classify whether a given text was generated by a large language model or written by a human. They are commonly used in academic, editorial, and content-moderation contexts to flag potentially AI-generated material. A key evaluation metric is the false-positive rate (FPR), which measures how often the detector incorrectly labels human text as AI-generated; a low FPR is essential for practical usability.

## [OpenAI's Astra Model Reaches Critical Cybersecurity Threshold](https://t.me/zaihuapd/43571) ⭐️ 8.0/10

OpenAI is releasing Astra, claimed to be the first model to reach a critical cybersecurity threshold, achieving a 100% score on ExploitBench and discovering two zero-day vulnerabilities during internal testing. The model also shows improved safety alignment with a 91.5% rejection rate for jailbreak attempts, up from 59% with GPT-5.6 Sol. This development marks a significant milestone in AI cybersecurity capabilities, as models reaching the critical threshold can autonomously discover and exploit vulnerabilities in well-protected systems without human guidance. It raises important questions about AI safety, dual-use risks, and the need for robust monitoring frameworks as AI systems become more capable. Astra's advanced cybersecurity capabilities will initially be limited to a small group of testers, with OpenAI implementing Chain of Thought monitoring and security response triggers to review and interrupt high-risk activities. The model's performance was evaluated on ExploitBench, a five-tier capability ladder benchmark built by CMU researchers with Bugcrowd that tests exploitation against real, patched V8 vulnerabilities.

telegram · zaihuapd · Sep 2, 16:30

**Background**: OpenAI's Preparedness Framework, introduced in late 2023, classifies AI models across four capability tiers for potential harms: Low, Medium, High, and Critical. The critical threshold represents models with unprecedented cybersecurity capabilities that pose serious dual-use risks, as they can autonomously discover and exploit vulnerabilities. ExploitBench measures how far AI agents can climb a capability ladder from reaching vulnerable code to achieving arbitrary code execution across real-world vulnerabilities.
