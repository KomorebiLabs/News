## [OpenAI Claims AI Solved Navier-Stokes Millennium Prize Problem](https://openai.com/index/navier-stokes-solution/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

OpenAI claims its internal AI system has produced a proof that the Navier-Stokes equations for fluid motion can develop a singularity in finite time, effectively solving one of the seven Millennium Prize Problems. The proof was generated by an internal model that OpenAI says was trained for less than two weeks. If verified, this would mark the first time an AI system has solved a Millennium Prize Problem, representing a major milestone in AI-assisted mathematical research. It also raises critical questions about research integrity, attribution, and the future dynamics of mathematical discovery in the AI era. The claim has sparked significant community debate about its validity, with concerns about attribution and whether the work was allegedly based on someone else's actual research and prompts. Terence Tao observed that even rumors of someone working on a problem can trigger massive AI-powered effort to flatten it before original research reaches its full potential.

hackernews · tedsanders · Sep 8, 17:13 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49613262)

**Background**: The Navier-Stokes equations are fundamental partial differential equations that describe the motion of viscous fluid substances. The existence and smoothness of solutions to these equations in three dimensions is one of the seven Millennium Prize Problems, each carrying a $1 million prize from the Clay Mathematics Institute. This problem asks whether smooth initial conditions always lead to smooth solutions, or whether singularities—infinite velocities—can develop in finite time.

**Discussion**: The community discussion features Terence Tao's observations on AI's impact on research culture, allegations about the work's origins potentially being based on someone else's research, and substantive debate on AI's mathematical capabilities versus natural science. Some commenters express concern about research integrity and attribution, while others note the astounding capability demonstrated even if limited to mathematics.

## [NeurIPS Desk-Rejects 178 Papers via Flawed AI Detector](https://www.reddit.com/r/MachineLearning/comments/1wakf62/neurips_deskrejected_178_papers_for_being/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

NeurIPS desk-rejected 178 position paper submissions using the proprietary Pangram AI detector, which also flagged the track chairs' own papers at 24-69% AI-generated. This incident highlights serious flaws in using black-box AI detectors for academic peer review, risking false accusations against researchers and undermining trust in conference integrity. Pangram's default threshold initially flagged 42.7% of submissions, but chairs lowered it to achieve a 12.7% flag rate; 22 papers were rejected solely because their detector score exceeded 0.5 despite author denials.

reddit · r/MachineLearning · /u/tughanbulut · Sep 8, 10:19

**Background**: AI text detectors like Pangram use machine learning to distinguish human-written from AI-generated text, but their accuracy is debated, with false positives affecting non-native English writers. NeurIPS is a premier machine learning conference that recently adopted this detector for desk-rejections without human review or appeal, sparking community backlash.
